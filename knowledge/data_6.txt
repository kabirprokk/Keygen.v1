HISTORY

Ancient and Classical Era
The abacus originated in ancient Mesopotamia around 2500 BCE as the earliest counting device. Ancient Egypt developed hieroglyphic writing and built pyramids as tombs for pharaohs. Ancient Greece contributed democracy in Athens, philosophy from Socrates, Plato, and Aristotle, and mathematics from Euclid and Pythagoras. The Roman Republic became the Roman Empire, which built roads, aqueducts, and codified Roman law. The fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 CE marked the beginning of the Middle Ages.

Medieval and Renaissance
The Islamic Golden Age preserved and expanded Greek and Roman knowledge through scholars like Ibn Sina and Al-Khwarizmi, who gave algebra its name. The Magna Carta of 1215 established the principle that everyone including the king is subject to the law. The Black Death killed one third of Europe's population in the 14th century. The Renaissance began in Italy in the 14th century, with Leonardo da Vinci making art and scientific sketches, Michelangelo sculpting and painting, and Galileo Galilei advancing astronomy and physics. Gutenberg's printing press around 1450 revolutionized information spread.

Early Modern
The Age of Exploration saw Columbus reach the Americas in 1492, Magellan's crew circumnavigate the globe, and the Columbian Exchange transferring plants, animals, diseases, and people between Old and New Worlds. The Scientific Revolution of the 16th and 17th centuries featured Copernicus proposing heliocentrism, Kepler discovering planetary motion laws, Galileo using the telescope, Newton formulating mechanics and gravitation, and Harvey describing blood circulation. The Enlightenment of the 18th century emphasized reason and individual rights, with Locke, Voltaire, Montesquieu, Rousseau, and Smith. The American Revolution (1775-1783) and the French Revolution (1789-1799) overthrew monarchies. The Industrial Revolution began in Britain around 1760, with steam engines from Watt, cotton mills, iron production, and railroads.

Modern and Contemporary
The 19th century saw abolition of slavery, Darwin's theory of evolution by natural selection, Mendel's genetics, Pasteur's germ theory, Lister's antiseptic surgery, Maxwell's electromagnetism, Mendeleev's periodic table, and the invention of the telephone by Bell and the light bulb by Edison. World War I (1914-1918) introduced trench warfare, tanks, and airplanes. The Russian Revolution of 1917 established the Soviet Union. The Great Depression began in 1929. World War II (1939-1945) included the Holocaust, use of atomic bombs, and the rise of the United States and Soviet Union as superpowers. The Cold War saw the space race, nuclear arms race, Korean War, Vietnam War, and the fall of the Berlin Wall in 1989. The digital age began with the first electronic computers in the 1940s, microprocessors in the 1970s, personal computers in the 1980s, the World Wide Web in the 1990s, and smartphones in the 2000s.

SCIENCE

Physics
Classical mechanics rests on Newton's three laws. First law: inertia, an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon. Second law: force equals mass times acceleration. Third law: action equals opposite reaction. Energy is conserved. Momentum is conserved. Work is force times displacement. Kinetic energy is one half mass times velocity squared. Gravitational potential energy is mass times gravity times height. Thermodynamics has four laws. Zeroth law establishes temperature. First law conserves energy: change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done. Second law states entropy never decreases in an isolated system. Third law says entropy approaches a constant as temperature approaches absolute zero. Electromagnetism is described by Maxwell's equations. Gauss's law for electricity relates electric flux to enclosed charge. Gauss's law for magnetism states no magnetic monopoles exist. Faraday's law says changing magnetic fields induce electric fields. Ampère's law with Maxwell's addition says magnetic fields come from currents and changing electric fields. Electromagnetic waves travel at the speed of light. The electromagnetic spectrum from low to high frequency includes radio, microwave, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X rays, and gamma rays.

Chemistry
Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons. The periodic table organizes elements by atomic number. Chemical bonds include ionic, covalent, and metallic. Organic chemistry studies carbon compounds. Functional groups determine reactivity. The hydroxyl group OH characterizes alcohols. Carbonyl groups appear in aldehydes and ketones. Carboxyl groups COOH characterize carboxylic acids. Amines contain nitrogen. Aromatic compounds contain benzene rings with delocalized pi electrons. The four major biomolecules are carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Carbohydrates include monosaccharides like glucose, disaccharides like sucrose, and polysaccharides like starch and cellulose. Lipids include triglycerides, phospholipids, and steroids. Proteins are polymers of twenty amino acids, folded into primary, secondary, alpha helix and beta sheet, tertiary, and quaternary structures. Enzymes catalyze reactions with active sites. Nucleic acids DNA and RNA store genetic information. DNA is a double helix with base pairing A to T and G to C. The central dogma is DNA to RNA to protein.

Biology and Medicine
Cell theory states all living things are made of cells, cells are the basic unit of life, and cells come from existing cells. Prokaryotes lack a nucleus. Eukaryotes have a nucleus and organelles including mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, Golgi apparatus, and lysosomes. Photosynthesis converts light energy into chemical energy in chloroplasts, producing oxygen and glucose. Cellular respiration breaks down glucose to produce ATP in mitochondria. Human body systems include integumentary skin, skeletal bones and joints, muscular skeletal muscles, nervous brain and nerves, endocrine hormones, cardiovascular heart and blood vessels, lymphatic immunity, respiratory lungs and airways, digestive alimentary canal and accessory organs, urinary kidneys and bladder, and reproductive gonads and genitalia. The brain has cerebrum with four lobes, cerebellum, and brainstem. The heart has four chambers and the sinoatrial node as natural pacemaker. Blood contains red blood cells with hemoglobin, white blood cells for immunity, and platelets for clotting. The immune system includes innate physical and cellular defenses and adaptive B cell and T cell responses.

Astronomy and Geology
The Big Bang occurred about 13.8 billion years ago. The universe is expanding, and expansion is accelerating due to dark energy. Dark matter is invisible matter detected only through gravity. Normal matter makes up only five percent of the universe. Stars fuse hydrogen into helium. Main sequence stars like the Sun become red giants then white dwarfs. High mass stars become supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes. The solar system formed 4.6 billion years ago. The inner terrestrial planets are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. The outer gas and ice giants are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune. Earth's interior has crust, mantle, outer core, and inner core. Plate tectonics drives continents, earthquakes, and volcanoes. The geologic time scale includes Hadean, Archean, Proterozoic, and Phanerozoic eons. The Phanerozoic has Paleozoic, Mesozoic, and Cenozoic eras.

TECHNOLOGY

Computing History
The Analytical Engine designed by Charles Babbage is considered the first computer concept. Ada Lovelace wrote the first computer program. Alan Turing proposed the Turing machine and broke the Enigma code. ENIAC was the first general purpose electronic digital computer. The stored program concept defines the von Neumann architecture. The transistor invented at Bell Labs replaced vacuum tubes. The integrated circuit allowed multiple transistors on one chip. The microprocessor, starting with Intel 4004, enabled personal computers. The Altair 8800, Apple II, and IBM PC brought computing to individuals. The graphical user interface was pioneered at Xerox PARC and popularized by Apple Macintosh and Microsoft Windows. The World Wide Web was created by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989.

Programming Languages
Fortran was the first high level language for scientific computing. Lisp was designed for artificial intelligence with symbolic computation. Cobol was for business data processing. Basic was for beginners. C was for systems programming and Unix. C++ added object oriented features. Java was designed for cross platform use with the Java Virtual Machine. Python emphasizes readability and became dominant in data science. JavaScript is the language of web browsers. Go and Rust are modern systems languages.

Artificial Intelligence
The Dartmouth workshop of 1956 founded AI as a field. Early AI focused on symbolic reasoning and search. Expert systems encoded human knowledge in rules. Machine learning algorithms learn from data. Deep learning uses neural networks with many layers. Convolutional neural networks excel at image recognition. Recurrent neural networks and Transformers excel at sequences and language. Large language models like GPT are trained on massive text corpora. Reinforcement learning achieved superhuman performance in games like Go. AI applications include computer vision, natural language processing, robotics, and autonomous vehicles.

GEOGRAPHY

Physical Geography
Earth's surface is about seventy one percent water and twenty nine percent land. The five oceans are Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, and Arctic. The seven continents are Asia, Africa, North America, South America, Antarctica, Europe, and Australia. Major mountain ranges include the Himalayas with Mount Everest, the Andes, the Rockies, the Alps, and the Urals. Major rivers include the Nile longest, Amazon largest volume, Yangtze, Mississippi, and Yenisei. Major deserts include the Sahara largest hot desert, Arabian, Gobi, and Kalahari. Climate zones include tropical, dry, temperate, continental, and polar. Biomes include rainforest, grassland, desert, taiga, tundra, and temperate forest.

Human Geography
The world population exceeds eight billion people. The most populous countries are China, India, the United States, Indonesia, Pakistan, Nigeria, Brazil, Bangladesh, Russia, and Mexico. Major languages by number of speakers are Mandarin Chinese, Spanish, English, Hindi, Arabic, Portuguese, Bengali, Russian, Japanese, and Punjabi. Major religions include Christianity, Islam, Hinduism, Buddhism, and Judaism. The largest cities by population include Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, Sao Paulo, Mexico City, Cairo, Dhaka, Mumbai, Beijing, and Osaka. The United Nations is the primary international organization founded after World War II. The European Union is a political and economic union of European countries.
